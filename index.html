Presentation guidelines

Treat this like a briefing to an executive team, not a class report.

Duration

  • 15 to 20 minutes
  • Results and recommendations take the most time
  • Rehearse with the timer on this page

Format

  • A professional business presentation
  • Simple language, minimal jargon
  • Every chart titled and labeled, with units

Delivery

  • All group members participate equally
  • Plan who presents each section before you rehearse
  • Practice handoffs between speakers

Presentation structure

Eight sections, in this order. The bar is sized by suggested time; select a segment to jump to that section.

Problem, question, data, methodsResults, recommendations, close
1Opening slideTitle, names, date, and a hook1 min
  • Project title
  • Group member names
  • Date
  • A brief hook: why should the audience care about your problem?
2Business problem and contextWhy is this analysis important?2–3 min
  • Brief organizational or industry background
  • A clear statement of the business problem
  • Why the problem matters: stakeholders and impact
  • Who will benefit from your analysis
Key question to answerWhy is this analysis important?
3Research question and hypothesesWhat you’re testing, in plain words1–2 min
  • Your specific research question
  • Null hypothesis (H₀)
  • Alternative hypothesis (Hₐ)
  • The target value or benchmark being tested
Presentation tipUse simple language and avoid excessive jargon.
4Data descriptionWhere the data came from and what it looks like2–3 min
  • Data source and collection method
  • Sample size (n)
  • Time period
  • Key descriptive statistics: mean, standard deviation, median
  • Visual: a histogram or box plot of the distribution

Key talking points

  • Why is this data appropriate for the problem?
  • Any data limitations or quality considerations
5Statistical methodologyWhy these methods fit, without the weeds2–3 min
  • Why you chose a confidence interval plus a hypothesis test
  • Key assumptions and how you verified them: normality, independence, and sample size adequacy
  • Your significance level (α = 0.05) and why
Presentation tipDon’t get bogged down in technical details. Focus on why these methods are appropriate.
6Results and findingsThe core of your presentation4–5 min

6a. Confidence interval 2 min

  • Present your 95% confidence interval clearly
  • Visual: an error bar plot or CI visualization
  • Interpretation: “We are 95% confident that the true population [variable] is between X and Y.”

6b. Hypothesis test results 2–3 min

  • Test statistic value
  • P-value
  • Decision: reject or fail to reject H₀
  • Visual: a distribution plot showing the test statistic, or a comparison chart
  • Your statistical conclusion in plain language
Example“Our analysis provides strong evidence that average customer satisfaction (7.4) differs significantly from the target of 7.5 (p = 0.03).”

6c. Practical significance

  • What does this mean in business terms?
  • Is the statistical difference practically meaningful?
  • Quantify the impact if possible
7Business implications and recommendationsSo what should we do about this?3–4 min
  • What the findings mean for the organization
  • Specific, actionable recommendations
  • Different scenarios, if appropriate
  • Honest acknowledgment of limitations
  • Next steps or future analysis
Key question to answerSo what should we do about this?
8ConclusionRecap and close strong1 min

Briefly recap the problem, your key finding, and your main recommendation. End with a strong closing statement that ties back to business impact.

Required visuals

At minimum, your slides need these four.

1Data distributionA histogram or box plot, in the data description
2Confidence intervalAn error bar or CI plot, in the results
3Hypothesis test resultA comparison chart or distribution plot, in the results
4Summary results tableDescriptive statistics: n, mean, standard deviation, median
One idea per slideTitle each slide with its takeaway, such as “Satisfaction is below target,” not just “Results.”
Translate the numbersSay what a p-value or interval means for the business, not just its value.
Make charts readableLarge fonts, labeled axes with units, and the target or benchmark marked on the chart.
Know your limitationsName them before someone else asks. It builds credibility.
